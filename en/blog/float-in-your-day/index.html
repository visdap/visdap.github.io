Float: a project management idea your day is missing

October 31, 2026

Project schedules have float. Your own day usually doesn't.

If you're preparing for the PMP, you know float: how long a task can slip without moving the finish date. Tasks with zero float sit on the critical path, and those are the ones you watch.

Now apply it to Tuesday. Work, commute, dinner, then 90 minutes of study, then bed. On paper that plan is complete. It also has zero float. One late meeting, one slow train, one child who won't sleep, and the study block is the thing that goes.

Find your real float

Draw tomorrow on a 24-hour circle and shade only the fixed blocks: sleep, work, travel, anything you can't move. What's left is your real float.

Now place your study block. If it sits right up against a hard stop, it's on the critical path, and it will slip first.

Three ways to add some

Move the study block away from the hard stop, even by 30 minutes. A block that starts after dinner and ends well before bed can survive a late start.

Schedule a backup slot. A second, shorter block on the same day or the next one means a missed session doesn't become a missed week.

Plan fewer sessions. Four that really happen beat six that exist only on paper.

Project managers plan buffers for everyone else's work. Yours deserves one too.
