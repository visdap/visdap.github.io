How many times were you interrupted today?

October 31, 2026

Say a question takes five minutes. Finding your place again can take about as long, and nobody counts that part.

"Can you take a quick look at this?" "Where's that file?" "I need the numbers before lunch." In many offices a dozen of these in a day is normal, and each one really is small. That's the trap. Small things don't feel like they need a plan.

The cost shows up later. If you spent five minutes answering and another five finding the line you stopped on, ten interruptions took a hundred minutes out of the day, and no calendar shows them. The work you meant to do in the morning moves to the evening, and it feels like you just didn't get to it.

See the day in pieces

Try this tomorrow. Draw a circle with 24 hours around the edge and shade your working hours. Each time someone breaks your concentration, draw a thin line through the shaded part at that time.

By evening you won't have a long block anymore. You'll have a row of short pieces, and you can count how many were long enough for the hard task. For many people the honest answer is one or two.

Two habits that help

Protect two hours in the morning: no calls, no chat, and a clear signal to your colleagues that this is the time for it.

Then collect every "just a minute" into one block after lunch. People rarely mind waiting a few hours when they know when the answer will come.

The point isn't to stop helping. It's to help at a time you chose.
